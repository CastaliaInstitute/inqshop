A workshop for inquiry

A studio for the mind — and a garage for the hand.

iNQshop is the physical making complement to iNQspace. It supports craft, fabrication, observation, measurement, and publication across the whole educational arc: from early cardboard play through advanced fabrication and research-grade prototyping. This site holds the public concept brief: vision, curriculum architecture, one-car-garage layout, equipment and safety, how the site fits the broader iNQspace map, blueprint offerings, and a phased rollout.

Vision and design goals

The room should read as a hybrid: part atelier, part laboratory, part tool library, and part family workshop. The core experience is that ideas become artifacts. For a garage-based version, the design priority is to preserve the existing square center bench, keep the overhead garage door unobstructed, and create a safer step-up working surface for smaller children instead of movable stools.

Design goals

  • Retain the center bench as the pedagogical heart of the room.
  • Provide a supervised step-up platform for smaller children rather than loose stools.
  • Place heavier, noisier, dusty, or fume-producing equipment on the perimeter walls.
  • Keep the garage-door opening and circulation path clear.
  • Support multiple modes at once: cardboard construction, painting, woodwork, digital fabrication, notebook work, and display.
  • Make the room legible to children: tools, materials, exemplars, and project stages visible and named.

Curriculum architecture (K–PhD+)

Use the same project families at increasing depth, not a separate project list for every age: observe → make → measure → model → explain → publish → teach.

Strand Seed / Apprentice Builder / Studio Collegium / Magisterium / PhD+
Form & structure Towers, bridges, shelters, shape hunts Trusses, joints, scale drawings, load tests Parametric structures, material optimization, publishable studies
Motion & mechanism Ramps, wheels, marble runs, automata Linkages, gears, cams, motorized builds Controls, robotics, mechanism synthesis
Light & image Shadows, color, pinhole viewers Optics, projection, stop-motion, scanning Computational imaging, AR/VR interfaces
Matter & materials Texture, paint, glue, clay, paper Material comparisons, 3D print studies, laser settings Characterization, sustainability, materials informatics
Nature & life Habitats, nests, seeds, weather tools Biomimicry, sensing, ecology builds Bio-inspired engineering, field instruments
Civilization & artifact Masks, maps, costumes, story boxes Historical reconstructions, models, exhibit builds Research installations, public humanities artifacts

Core outputs at every level

  • Artifact — the built thing
  • Notebook — sketches, measurements, reflections
  • Model — CAD, diagram, theory, or simulation
  • Demonstration — show how it works
  • Explanation — oral or written defense
  • Publication — portfolio page, poster, paper, or guide
  • Teaching — hand the knowledge downward to a younger student

One-car garage concept plan

Concept layout for a typical footprint of roughly 12 ft × 20 ft. Not a permit drawing: adapt to measurements, door swings, outlets, egress, and local code. The intent is zoning, circulation, and curriculum use.

Spatial logic

  • Center bench — shared surface for cardboard, drawing, notebooks, assembly, supervised hand-tool work.
  • Step-up — fixed or semi-fixed surface (~10–12 in high, anti-slip, rounded edges) along selected bench sides so smaller children reach bench height without individual stools.
  • Left wall — softer, messier, child-forward zones: cardboard, adhesives, paint, drying racks, open cubbies.
  • Right wall — fabrication edge: 3D printers (vented), laser (vented), CNC/wood bench with dust control.
  • Back wall — teaching and reference: teacher station, materials library, peg rail, display, current-work boards.
  • Garage door — keep visually and physically clear; no tall storage or carts in that path.

Center bench Retain existing; enlarge if practical Square plan preferred for group work
Step-up platform 10–12 in high, 12–18 in deep Supervised; anti-slip; rounded edges
Main circulation At least 36 in clear More if carts pass
Laser enclosure Dedicated wall bay Vent outdoors if possible

Equipment, storage, safety, utilities

Zone Key contents
Cardboard construction Corrugate stock, templates, child-safe cutters, metal rulers, hole punches, tabs/slots; hot-glue for adult use only
Paint and wet media Washable paints, palettes, aprons, drying rack, wipe-clean mat; sink or utility tub if available
Wood and hand tools Age-appropriate saws, clamps, bench hooks, sanding zone, offcut bins
Digital fabrication 3D printers, laser, CNC or desktop mill, calibration drawer, maintenance kit
Notebook and design Graph paper, field notebooks, pencils, calipers, scales, examples, build instructions
Display and archive Finished-work shelf, project rail, labeled bins, samples, exemplar board

Safety baseline

  • Separate child-accessible tools from adult-only cutting, heat, and machine tools.
  • Vented enclosures for laser and preferably for 3D printing.
  • Dust collection for CNC and sanding; fine dust should not share air casually with paint or toddler activity.
  • Fire extinguisher, first-aid, eye and hearing protection, clear shutdown procedures visible.
  • Labeled shadow-board storage for visual check-in/out.
  • Treat the step-up as supervised infrastructure, not a play platform.

Blueprint packages

Offer reproducible plans as concept sets, not stamped construction documents. Each package should include: cover and audience; measured plan; wall elevations; bench and step-up details; utilities and ventilation concept; safety and supervision; sample curriculum sheet; purchasing/spec with good–better–best tiers.

Package Audience Contents / use case
Starter garage Homeschool families Floor plan, storage elevations, tool list, safety checklist, curriculum starter — convert one bay quickly
Toddler + builder Mixed ages Bench/step-up, child zones, washable materials, low-risk tool progression — shared family workshop
Fabrication garage Older learners / micro-school Laser/CNC/3D enclosures, power/vent, advanced tool list — small but serious makerspace
Mobile pop-up Co-op / temporary Fold-flat carts, portable surfaces, transport list — deployable workshop
Studio + shop hybrid Art + fabrication Paint wall, print wall, book arts, digital edge — balanced atelier

Phased implementation roadmap

  1. Phase 1 — Clear and zone. Paint, lighting, wall storage, center bench retention, cardboard and notebook capability.
  2. Phase 2 — Child-ready making. Step-up surface, aprons, drying racks, labeled bins, safe hand-tool set.
  3. Phase 3 — Fabrication edge. 3D printers, laser enclosure, dust-aware wood/CNC bench, maintenance storage.
  4. Phase 4 — Curriculum publishing. Project ladder, blueprint product line, iNQspace pages, exemplar builds.

The strength of this design is coherence: the room, the curriculum, and the web front-end all say the same thing — learning should result in artifacts, evidence, and living competence. The garage becomes a visible node in the Castalia ecology: iNQspace online, iNQshop in the world.
